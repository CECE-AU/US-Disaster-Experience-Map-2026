Research Data: Surveys designed by CECE and administered with YouGov to a nationally representative sample (Oct 2025 & 2026); Observed hazard event counts compiled from the NOAA NCEI Storm Events Database (raw CSV data).

Methodology & Data Sources

1. Data Sources & Recall Windows:
  • Survey Experience Data: Nationally representative surveys (n = 1,500 per wave) designed by the American University Center for Environment, Community, and Equity (CECE) and administered by YouGov in October 2025 (6-month recall window: May 1 – October 31, 2025) and October 2026 (12-month recall window: November 1, 2025 – October 31, 2026). Note: comparing 2025 to 2026 compares a 6-month recall period against a 12-month recall period.
  • Observed Events Data: Documented storm and climate hazard events from the NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information (NCEI) Storm Events Database, matched to the exact 2025 (May – Oct 2025) and 2026 (Nov 2025 – Oct 2026) survey windows and weighted by 2025 U.S. Census Bureau county populations.
2. Survey Experience Layer (%): State percentages for each disaster category are calculated using the exact number of valid responses to that specific question in that state (nquestion):
State Percentage = (Respondents Experiencing Hazard / Valid Responses to Question) × 100
3. Observed Events Layer (Population-Weighted Events per Resident): Because NCEI logs a separate entry for every county or forecast zone an event crosses, raw state event sums over-represent geographically large states with many counties. To measure the number of events experienced by the average resident in each state, county event counts are weighted by each county’s share of the state population:
Pop-Weighted Events per Resident = Σ(EventsCounty × PopulationCounty) / PopulationState
  • Any Disaster (Overall): Total population-weighted events across all five categories below.
  • Extreme Heat: NCEI Excessive Heat and Heat episodes.
  • Flooding: NCEI Flash Flood, Flood, Coastal Flood, Lakeshore Flood, and Storm Surge/Tide episodes.
  • Drought / Water Shortage: NCEI Drought episodes.
  • Wildfires / Smoke: NCEI Wildfire and Dense Smoke episodes.
  • More Frequent and Powerful Storms: NCEI Hurricane (Typhoon), Tornado, Tropical Storm, Tropical Depression, Thunderstorm Wind, High Wind, Strong Wind, Hail, Lightning, Funnel Cloud, and Dust Storm episodes.
4. Classification & Shifts: "Natural Breaks (Jenks)" pools 2025 and 2026 state values per disaster category into 5 variance-minimizing classes so color bins stay locked when toggling between years. "Interval/Scale" uses fixed 20% increments on Survey views, equal intervals on Observed Event views, and a 0-centered diverging scale on 2025–2026 shift maps.
